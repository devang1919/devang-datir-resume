A low-friction improvement

A phased approach suited to a small shop
1
Bring records together

Maintain one spreadsheet for product, quantity, sale date, supplier and purchase date.

2
Set a reorder threshold

Mark products that drop below a minimum quantity for the next order.

3
Review a weekly snapshot

Check fast sellers, stock-outs, high-sales days and late deliveries weekly.

Source classifications

Two broad ways information is gathered
D
Documentary sources

Existing records such as sales bills, billing reports, stock registers and purchase records.

H
Human / non-documentary sources

Information gained through customers, conversations, feedback and the owner's knowledge.

Why classification matters

The source should fit the question

Sales bills show what was purchased, but may not explain why a customer chose a product. Combining records with customer conversations gives both behavioural evidence and context.

Step 05 · Collect evidence

Collection methods

Choose methods that answer the question with the least friction and the most reliable evidence.

METHOD 01
Analysis of existing records

Review sales bills or billing software reports to identify fast-selling products. Existing records give factual transaction evidence without asking customers to remember purchases.

Best forFast-selling products
METHOD 02
Questionnaire

Ask customers a short, consistent set of questions about satisfaction, availability and service. Responses can be compared rather than relying on a few memorable conversations.

Best forCustomer satisfaction
METHOD 03
Observation

Check shelves and compare quantities with the stock register. Repeated checks reveal low stock quickly and fit the owner's normal daily routine.

Best forProducts to reorder

Personal reflection

Business justification

Why organised information creates value

Proper records make the business easier to manage. Instead of depending only on memory, organised information can help the owner track sales, stock, suppliers and customer feedback. This can reduce mistakes, save time, improve service and make future planning easier. It can also help the shop understand buying trends and avoid running out of popular products.

From records to better decisions

Recommended next steps

Simple changes, meaningful impact
1
Create one master register

Combine sales trends, stock counts, purchases and supplier details in one searchable place.

2
Use simple reorder indicators

Define minimum stock levels for frequently purchased items.

3
Review patterns weekly

Compare sales by product and day, check supplier delays and review customer feedback.
